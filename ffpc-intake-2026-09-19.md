## Part 2 — the six scored sections

The written application is the only gate: 16 teams of roughly 70 advance on these answers alone.
Each is capped at 2,000 characters in the form, so a long answer here is better than a short one.
The agent cuts.

Judges score every section against eight criteria: Problem/Opportunity, Solution, Innovativeness,
Achievability, Scalability, Fundability, Persuasiveness, and Presentation (presentation does not
apply to the application round).

**2.1 PROBLEM / MARKET OPPORTUNITY.** What breaks today, and for whom. The form asks specifically for
the size and scope of the problem, **how you became aware of it**, and what inspired you to start the
company. The second part is the one an agent cannot invent, so it matters most here.

ANSWER:

**2.2 SOLUTION / PRODUCT / SERVICE.** What you would build and how it works, concentrating on the
value proposition and the customer's benefit. Include anything that already exists: a prototype,
code, a design, a conversation with a potential user.

ANSWER:

**2.3 POTENTIAL RETURN / REVENUE MODEL.** Who pays, and roughly what. The form wants the pricing
model, expected profit margin, fixed and variable costs, key resources you have and still need, plus
market size and growth rate. The agent researches market size and growth. Andrew supplies the
pricing intuition and who holds the budget.

ANSWER:

**2.4 COMPETITION ANALYSIS.** Who solves this today and why you win, plus external risks to the
business. "People do it manually in a spreadsheet" is a legitimate competitor and should be named as
one. List anyone known, even roughly.

ANSWER:

**2.5 EXECUTION PLAN / GO-TO-MARKET.** Sales, marketing, development, and partnership plans, with
profiles of target customers, milestones already hit, and milestones planned. Concretely: who are
the first ten users, how are they reached, and what has already been done toward the idea
(conversations, waitlist, prototype, anything).

ANSWER:

**2.6 THE TEAM / RELEVANT EXPERIENCE.** Solo or co-founders. If co-founders: names, majors,
classifications, roles, and what each brings. Up to three presenters, all UT Austin undergraduates.
Also: any other business-plan competitions or entrepreneurship programs anyone on the team has
participated in. The agent will draft Andrew's own paragraph from the repo (Square Kettle as a
founding member, the $8M raise, 13 interns, Amazon, SpaceX, Apple) unless told otherwise.

ANSWER:
