# FFPC: who judges it, what wins it, and where to point an idea

Research compiled 2026-09-19 for the Freed Family Pitch Competition at UT Austin. Every claim below
carries the link it came from. Competition rules, deadlines and prize mechanics live in
`README.md`; this file is only about the audience and the track record.

---

## Executive overview

Four years of results point one direction. Across 2022 through 2025, twenty startups placed or won
an award, and not one was financial infrastructure, developer tooling, or a trading business. The
winners cluster in health and medical devices, climate and waste, education, and campus consumer
products, and the single most reliable trait among first-place finishers is a **physical or visible
thing the judges can watch work**: a firefighting robot ([2022](https://ut-kelleher-ent.medium.com/recap-rewind-disruptexas-undergrad-pitch-competition-finals-2022-d4f599b70850)),
a $30 eye-exam instrument ([2024](https://thedailytexan.com/2024/10/22/ut-engineering-students-win-freed-family-pitch-competition-with-all-eyes-uniquely-accessible-vision-care-instrument/)),
summer camps with real enrolled students ([2023](https://ut-kelleher-ent.medium.com/recap-freed-family-pitch-competition-finals-2023-83c3586ba194)).
The judging panel explains why. Of the fifteen finals and elevator judges HKEC has named since 2022,
the backgrounds are restaurants, hotels, spirits, chewing gum, pet products, oil and gas, IP
litigation consulting, emergency-communications software, video games, small-business acquisition,
and two generalist finance executives. Zero come from quantitative finance, and only one, Ecliptic
Capital's [David J. Neff](https://texasventurealliance.org/member-profiles/david-neff), invests in
deep tech for a living. These are operators who have sold a physical product to a real customer, and
the official criteria they score on reward exactly that: Achievability, Scalability, and, in the
elevator round, "sound evidence for market validation"
([rule book](https://utexas.box.com/s/cl23k9o14qidnbukzouwzl5817y2gbn7)).

**The direction: pick a problem you have personally run into, that a non-technical adult recognizes
in one sentence, where you can show something working on stage and name the first customer who
pays.** Take the technical depth as the moat, not the pitch. The reasoning is mechanical rather than
aesthetic. The written round scores six sections in which two of the six (revenue model, go-to-market)
are pure commercial questions that a research-flavored idea cannot answer, so an idea with no named
payer loses points before anyone sees you present. Judges who built Hopdoddy's finance function or a
20,000-store gum brand will probe unit economics and distribution, not model architecture. The
finals are public, recorded, and carry a $1,000 audience vote in a 300-seat auditorium, so
legibility to a room of students, parents and staff is worth real money. And the origin-story
question is scored: the application asks outright how you became aware of the problem, and the
pattern among winners is a first-person answer, from Rev Gum's founder being diagnosed with Type 1
diabetes as a UT chemistry major
([Statesman](https://www.statesman.com/story/business/2019/12/04/austins-rev-gum-wants-to-amp-you-up-with-caffeinated-chewing-gum/2158047007/))
to the AllEyes team interviewing patients in Vietnam and India after a freshman biodesign program
([Daily Texan](https://thedailytexan.com/2024/10/22/ut-engineering-students-win-freed-family-pitch-competition-with-all-eyes-uniquely-accessible-vision-care-instrument/)).

## Part 1 — The judges

HKEC names its finals judges in each year's recap and announces finals judges on social media in the
weeks before the event; application-round and semi-finals judges are never announced
([rule book](https://utexas.box.com/s/cl23k9o14qidnbukzouwzl5817y2gbn7)). The panel rotates every
year, so treat these as the *type* of person who judges, not the list you will face.

## Part 3 — What this implies for choosing an idea

### The six-part test

Score a candidate idea out of 6. Anything under 4 is an uphill written round.

| Test | Why it matters here | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A non-technical adult gets it in one sentence.** | Judges are restaurateurs, hoteliers and distillers; the audience votes $1,000 | Judge table above |
| **There is something to show.** | Live physical demos are allowed in finals and elevator rounds with prior approval, virtual demos in semi-finals and finals | [Rule book](https://utexas.box.com/s/cl23k9o14qidnbukzouwzl5817y2gbn7) |
| **You met the problem yourself.** | The application asks how you became aware of it and what inspired you | Live form; Rev Gum and AllEyes origin stories |
| **You can name who pays and how much.** | Required section asks pricing, margin, fixed and variable costs, market size, growth rate | Live form |
| **One number a judge repeats.** | "$30 versus $3,500", "6 tons a day" | AllEyes and Renew Robotics above |
| **A first customer exists or is one conversation away.** | Achievability and Market Validation are scored criteria | [Rule book](https://utexas.box.com/s/cl23k9o14qidnbukzouwzl5817y2gbn7) |

### Applied to the two ideas already on file

From [docs/hackathon-stories.md](hackathon-stories.md).

**Physics-based 3D world with swarms of AI agents.** Scores well on "something to show", since a
running simulation on a screen is a demo, and well on originality. It fails the payer test outright:
there is no customer, no price, and no market-size figure, which leaves two of the six required
written sections unanswerable and hits Achievability, Scalability and Fundability at once. As a
competition entry this is the weaker of the two unless it is repointed at a buyer, for example
training or evaluation environments sold to a named type of team.

**AI for generating vaccine candidates.** Legible mission, and the market size is easy to source.
It fails the achievability test for a solo undergraduate with no wet lab, and the judges most likely
to probe it are the ones with the least biotech background. The honest version of this pitch
requires a research partner, which the timeline does not allow before Oct 23.

### Where a stronger idea would come from

The archive rewards a specific shape: a problem the founder personally hit, in health, education,
waste, or campus life, with a cheap physical or visible artifact and one memorable number. Andrew's
unfair advantages, agent infrastructure, ML systems, and a founder track record including a raise,
are best used as **the reason this team can build it**, stated in the team section, rather than as
the pitch itself. The team section is the one place where Square Kettle belongs in this application,
and a raise of $8M from 13 investors is a strong answer to that section.

### Two related shots at the same money

**Forty Acres Founders** is HKEC's other program, a semester-long pre-accelerator rather than a
one-night competition. Applications open **September 23, 2026** and close **October 16, 2026**, it
runs January to April, it pays **$1,000 per founder** plus up to **$20,000** in an end-of-semester
pitch competition, and it carries the same 50%-equity founding-member rule. It is open to graduate
students as well as undergraduates, and it requires enrollment across both Fall and Spring
([Forty Acres Founders](https://herbkellehercenter.mccombs.utexas.edu/faf/)). Bloom Technologies
placed in both, so entering one does not preclude the other.

**Preliminary feedback before you submit** is free through the Harkey Institute's Undergraduate
Mentor Program, which the FFPC FAQ names for exactly this purpose
([Harkey mentor program](https://www.mccombs.utexas.edu/centers-initiatives/harkey-institute-for-entrepreneurial-studies/undergraduate-mentor-program/)).
